## 1. Concept and context

The risk-neutral price at time $n$ of a contract paying $V_m$ at time $m$ is $V_n=\frac{1}{D_n}\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[D_mV_m]$ (Eq. 6.4.1, the same as (6.2.8)). When $D_m$ is random, computing this conditional expectation requires the *joint* conditional distribution of $D_m$ and $V_m$ under the risk-neutral measure, which makes the pricing of fixed income derivatives difficult (Section 6.4, p. 161). One way around this dilemma is to build interest rate models using *forward measures* rather than the risk-neutral measure: the term $D_m$ in (6.4.1) is used as a Radon-Nikodým derivative to change to a different measure, and once expectations are computed under that measure the term $D_m$ no longer appears. For each maturity $m$ this gives the $m$-forward measure $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$, under which the price of any asset paying at time $m$, denominated in units of the zero-coupon bond maturing at $m$ — its forward price — is a martingale. The section illustrates the idea by recomputing the caplet of Example 6.3.9 (Example 6.4.3) and by repeating all the computations of Examples 6.3.9 and 6.4.3 in a three-period Ho-Lee model (Example 6.4.4), whose numbers, unlike those of the earlier examples, are of the kind used in practical applications (pp. 160–168). The book notes that the full power of this approach is realized only in continuous-time models (Section 6.6, p. 173); the concept of forward measures is due to Jamshidian and to Geman, El Karoui and Rochet, and these measures underlie the so-called market models, or forward LIBOR models, of interest rates (Section 6.7, p. 174).

## 2. Formal definitions

* **$Z_{m,m}$**: for $m$ fixed with $1\le m\le N$, the random variable $Z_{m,m}=\frac{D_m}{B_{0,m}}$ (Eq. 6.4.2). Like $D_m$, it depends on only the first $m-1$ coin tosses (Example 6.4.3, p. 162).
* **$m$-forward measure $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$**: the probability measure defined by $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m(\omega)=Z_{m,m}(\omega)\tilde{\mathbb P}(\omega)$ for all $\omega\in\Omega$ (Definition 6.4.1, p. 161). $\tilde{\mathbb E}^m$ and $\tilde{\mathbb E}^m_n$ denote expectation and conditional expectation under $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$.
* **Radon-Nikodým derivative process $Z_{n,m}$**: following the development of Section 3.2, $Z_{n,m}=\tilde{\mathbb E}_nZ_{m,m}$, $n=0,1,\dots,m$ (Eq. 6.4.3, p. 161).
* **$\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$-conditional (transition) probabilities**: the conditional probabilities of getting $H$ and $T$ at each node of the interest rate tree under $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$, computed as in Example 6.2.1 (p. 163).
* **Ho-Lee model**: the interest rate at time $n$ is $R_n(\omega_1\dots\omega_n)=a_n+b_n\cdot\#H(\omega_1\dots\omega_n)$, where $a_0,a_1,\dots$ and $b_1,b_2,\dots$ are constants used to calibrate the model (i.e., make the prices generated by the model agree with market data), and the risk-neutral probabilities are taken to be $\tilde p=\tilde q=\frac12$ (Example 6.4.4, p. 165).

## 3. Key equations

$$V_n=\frac{1}{D_n}\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[D_mV_m],\quad n=0,1,\dots,m \quad \text{(6.4.1)}$$

$$Z_{m,m}=\frac{D_m}{B_{0,m}} \quad \text{(6.4.2)}$$

$$Z_{n,m}=\tilde{\mathbb E}_nZ_{m,m},\quad n=0,1,\dots,m \quad \text{(6.4.3)}$$

$$\tilde{\mathbb E}^mV_m=\tilde{\mathbb E}[Z_{m,m}V_m] \quad \text{(6.4.4)}$$

$$\tilde{\mathbb E}^m_n[V_m]=\frac{1}{Z_{n,m}}\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[Z_{m,m}V_m] \quad \text{(6.4.5)}$$

$$Z_{n,m}=\frac{D_nB_{n,m}}{B_{0,m}},\quad n=0,\dots,m \quad \text{(6.4.6)}$$

$$\tilde{\mathbb E}^m[V_m]=\frac{1}{B_{0,m}}\tilde{\mathbb E}[D_mV_m] \quad \text{(6.4.7)}$$

$$\tilde{\mathbb E}^m_n[V_m]=\frac{1}{D_nB_{n,m}}\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[D_mV_m],\quad n=0,1,\dots,m \quad \text{(6.4.8)}$$

$$\tilde{\mathbb E}^m_n[V_m]=\frac{V_n}{B_{n,m}},\quad n=0,1,\dots,m \quad \text{(6.4.9)}$$

*Unnumbered formulas in the text:*

The check that $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$ is a probability measure, using the definition (6.2.3) of zero-coupon bond prices (p. 161):

$$\tilde{\mathbb P}^m(\Omega)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\tilde{\mathbb P}^m(\omega)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}Z_{m,m}(\omega)\tilde{\mathbb P}(\omega)=\tilde{\mathbb E}Z_{m,m}=\frac{1}{B_{0,m}}\tilde{\mathbb E}D_m=1$$

The time-zero price of the time-three caplet of Example 6.3.9 recovered through (6.4.9) (Example 6.4.3, p. 165):

$$V_0=B_{0,3}\tilde{\mathbb E}^3[V_3]=\frac47\cdot\frac16=\frac2{21}$$

## 4. Assumptions and domain of validity

* **Fixed maturity:** $m$ is fixed with $1\le m\le N$, and the payoff $V_m$ depends only on the first $m$ coin tosses (Definition 6.4.1 and Theorem 6.4.2, pp. 161–162). All the model assumptions of Section 6.2 remain in force.
* **One forward measure per payment date:** the $m$-forward measure is useful for pricing only those securities that pay off at time $m$, not at other times (p. 162). Accordingly, Examples 6.4.3 and 6.4.4 use $\tilde{\mathbb P}^3$ only for the caplet paid at time three.
* **Well-defined change of measure:** $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$ is a probability measure because $\tilde{\mathbb E}Z_{m,m}=1$, which follows from (6.2.3) (p. 161). Since $D_m>0$ (condition (6.2.1)) and $B_{0,m}>0$, $Z_{m,m}>0$, so $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$ assigns strictly positive probability to every path, as the risk-neutral measure does.
* **Use of the Section 3.2 lemmas:** (6.4.4) and (6.4.5) are obtained "according to Lemma 3.2.5" and "according to Lemma 3.2.6" (p. 161). The book proved those lemmas for the actual and risk-neutral measures of a model with independent tosses; here they are applied with $\tilde{\mathbb P}$ in the role of the reference measure and $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$ in the role of the new measure, with conditional expectations in the sense of Definition 6.2.2.
* **What the forward measure removes:** the left-hand side of (6.4.8) does not require knowing the correlation between $V_m$ and $D_m$, only the conditional distribution of $V_m$ under $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$; it is therefore often easier to compute than $\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[D_mV_m]$ (p. 162).
* **Nondividend-paying assets:** the martingale property of forward prices under $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$ is stated for nondividend-paying assets (p. 162).

## 5. Theorems and proof outline

**Theorem 6.4.2 (Pricing under the forward measure):** let $m$ be fixed, with $1\le m\le N$, and let $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$ denote the $m$-forward measure. If $V_m$ is a random variable depending on only the first $m$ coin tosses, then (6.4.7) holds; more generally, if $V_m$ depends on only the first $m$ coin tosses, then (6.4.8) holds (p. 162).

*Proof outline:*
1. By Lemma 3.2.5, the change of measure gives $\tilde{\mathbb E}^mV_m=\tilde{\mathbb E}[Z_{m,m}V_m]$ (6.4.4); by Lemma 3.2.6, for $0\le n\le m$, $\tilde{\mathbb E}^m_n[V_m]=\frac{1}{Z_{n,m}}\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[Z_{m,m}V_m]$ (6.4.5).
2. With $Z_{m,m}$ defined by (6.4.2), equation (6.2.5) ($D_nB_{n,m}=\tilde{\mathbb E}_n[D_m]$) shows that $Z_{n,m}=\tilde{\mathbb E}_n\left[\frac{D_m}{B_{0,m}}\right]=\frac{D_nB_{n,m}}{B_{0,m}}$ (6.4.6).
3. Substituting (6.4.2) into (6.4.4) gives (6.4.7); substituting (6.4.2) and (6.4.6) into (6.4.5), the factors $B_{0,m}$ cancel and give (6.4.8).

**Forward prices are martingales under the forward measure (p. 162):** combining (6.4.8) with the risk-neutral pricing formula (6.4.1) gives (6.4.9). Thus $\tilde{\mathbb E}^m_n[V_m]$ is the price at time $n$ of any derivative security or asset paying $V_m$ at time $m$, *denominated in units of the zero-coupon bond maturing at time $m$* — the forward price of the security or asset given in Theorem 6.3.2. Applying iterated conditioning to the left-hand side of (6.4.9) shows that a price denominated this way is a martingale under $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$: $m$-forward prices of (nondividend-paying) assets are martingales under the forward measure $\tilde{\mathbb P}^m$.

## 6. Exercises in this section (and required examples)

* **Example 6.4.3 (Caplet under the 3-forward measure) (pp. 162–165):** set $m=3$ in Definition 6.4.1 and use the data of Example 6.3.9, where $B_{0,3}=\frac47$. Then
$Z_{3,3}(HH)=\frac{D_3(HH)}{B_{0,3}}=\frac74\cdot\frac37=\frac34$, $Z_{3,3}(HT)=\frac74\cdot\frac67=\frac32$, $Z_{3,3}(TH)=\frac74\cdot\frac47=1$, $Z_{3,3}(TT)=\frac74\cdot\frac47=1$,
and $\tilde{\mathbb E}Z_{3,3}=\frac34\cdot\frac13+\frac32\cdot\frac16+1\cdot\frac14+1\cdot\frac14=1$, as it should.
Table of $\tilde{\mathbb P}(\omega)$, $Z_{3,3}(\omega)$, $\tilde{\mathbb P}^3(\omega)$: $HHH$: $\frac29,\frac34,\frac16$; $HHT$: $\frac19,\frac34,\frac1{12}$; $HTH$: $\frac1{12},\frac32,\frac18$; $HTT$: $\frac1{12},\frac32,\frac18$; $THH$: $\frac16,1,\frac16$; $THT$: $\frac1{12},1,\frac1{12}$; $TTH$: $\frac18,1,\frac18$; $TTT$: $\frac18,1,\frac18$.
$\tilde{\mathbb P}^3$ transition probabilities (Figure 6.4.1): $\frac12,\frac12$ at time zero; $\frac12,\frac12$ at $R_1(H)$ and at $R_1(T)$; $\frac23,\frac13$ at $R_2(HH)$ and $R_2(TH)$; $\frac12,\frac12$ at $R_2(HT)$ and $R_2(TT)$.
For the caplet $V_3=(R_2-\frac13)^+$, which depends only on the first two tosses, $V_3(HH)=\frac23$ and $V_3=0$ otherwise, so $\tilde{\mathbb E}^3_2[V_3]=V_3$ and
$\tilde{\mathbb E}^3_1[V_3](H)=\frac12V_3(HH)+\frac12V_3(HT)=\frac13$, $\tilde{\mathbb E}^3_1[V_3](T)=\frac12V_3(TH)+\frac12V_3(TT)=0$, $\tilde{\mathbb E}^3[V_3]=\frac14V_3(HH)+\frac14V_3(HT)+\frac14V_3(TH)+\frac14V_3(TT)=\frac16$.

Finally, (6.4.9) gives $V_0=B_{0,3}\tilde{\mathbb E}^3[V_3]=\frac47\cdot\frac16=\frac2{21}$, in agreement with (6.3.10).
